Klaire Packs
Website brief · Phase 1 · Rev. 2

A site that sells the vision — and quietly starts to fund it.

The next version of the Klaire Packs website: a simple, static site — free to host on GitHub Pages — that tells Klaire's story, gives supporters two easy ways to give, shows openly where the money goes, and lets rehab centres register and order their packs at no cost. Every payment runs through Zeffy, so no money ever touches the website itself.

00

The one-minute version

Klaire Packs already has a live alpha and a clear, honest story. This next build keeps it static and free on GitHub Pages, but makes it do more at once — tell the story, take money two simple ways, show where it goes, publish the safety screening centres rely on, and let centres order for free.

Donations, pack sponsorships and clinic sign-ups run on Zeffy (0% platform fee), embedded into the pages. The donation form is already live. Nothing here needs a server, a login system, or a monthly bill — and no card data ever touches the site.

01

Where things stand

  • The org. Klaire Packs is an Australian unincorporated not-for-profit association (ABN 28 968 180 521). It is not ACNC-registered and holds no DGR status — so gifts are not tax-deductible. That single fact shapes how every "give" moment is worded (see §11).
  • There's a working alpha. Live on GitHub Pages with four pages — Home, The packs, For centres, Impact — and a complete design system already built.
  • Zeffy is set up, donation form is live. A genuinely 0%-fee platform for small non-profits. The one-off/recurring donation form already exists; the sponsorship shop and clinic forms are still to be created.
  • A live cost planner exists. A Google Sheet holds the pack contents and real cost-per-box figures — the source for the new Transparency page (§06).
  • Two carry-overs. The brand is moving from "Claire" to "Klaire" everywhere, and the domain is moving to klairepacks.org.
02

The vision the site has to carry

Get this right and the money follows; get it wrong and no widget will save it.

Klaire Packs exists in honour of Klaire — a sister who fought addiction with more courage than most people will ever know, and who was lost at 41. Every pack carries the small comforts someone wished had been handed to her. That's the emotional centre of the whole site, and it's real — so it should be given room, not over-produced.

The product is three care packs, each built for one stage of recovery and safe for a clinic setting. The cost figures below are the current planning estimates from the cost sheet:

Rehab centres receive all three free for a six-month trial. The site's emotional job is to make a stranger feel that a small, well-timed thing lands in a big moment. Its commercial job is to turn that feeling, gently, into a donation or a sponsored pack.

03

What "done" looks like at this stage

In scope — Phase 1
  • Sell the vision. A visitor understands the story, the packs and who they help in about a minute.
  • Open two income streams. Donations and pack sponsorships, both live and taking real money.
  • Earn trust openly. Publish a Transparency page (where money goes) and a Screening Rules page (why the packs are safe).
  • Keep serving centres. Clinics register and order at no cost, with as little friction as possible.
  • Stay free and simple. Static, on GitHub Pages, no ongoing fees, maintainable by one person.
Deliberately not yet

Real clinic logins, an accounts system, a CRM, or an automated live impact dashboard. All valuable — all Phase 2 (see §13). Adding them now would trade the site's biggest current asset, its simplicity, for machinery you don't need at a handful of centres.

04

Who the site is for

Three audiences, three jobs — the homepage has to serve all three without shouting.

Supporters — individuals

Moved by the story

The public who feel something reading about Klaire. Their job: give once, give monthly, or sponsor a pack. Lead with feeling, then make the ask effortless — one clear button, no forms to wade through.

Businesses & community groups

Want to give meaningfully

Workplaces, clubs, local businesses. Their job: sponsor packs in bulk (a whole cohort, a workplace fundraiser) or offer goods-in-kind. Give them a soft, low-friction "sponsor a cohort / get in touch" path.

Rehab & recovery centres

Put packs in hands

Intake coordinators, support workers, keyworkers. Their job: register, be reassured it's genuinely free and facility-safe (the Screening page is for them), and reorder easily. Speak plainly, give them one real contact.

The three ways to take part

All three run on Zeffy, embedded into the site.

Card entry, processing and receipts all happen inside Zeffy — the GitHub site only ever hosts a link or an iframe, so no payment data touches it and there's no PCI burden. Each mechanism below names the Zeffy product, where it sits, the wording that fits the brand, and its embed link (live or to-be-created).

Where
A dedicated Donate page, plus a compact button in the footer and at the end of the Impact story — the moment people are most moved.

5.2  Sponsor a pack — one, or all three

Zeffy · Shop / product form · TO CREATE

Recommendation up front: call it "Sponsor a pack," not "Buy a pack." A supporter isn't buying a product for themselves — they're funding a pack a clinic hands to someone in recovery. That framing fits the cause, gives the gift emotional weight, and sidesteps consumer-sale, returns and GST complications that "buying" would invite.

What
A small Zeffy shop with three items — sponsor a Welcome, a Steady or an Onward — plus "Sponsor the full journey" (all three) and an optional "Sponsor a cohort" quantity for businesses.
Pricing
Grounded in the real cost-per-box (Welcome ≈ $28, Steady ≈ $33, Onward ≈ $38, all three ≈ $99), plus a small margin for postage and overheads. Indicative tiers: Welcome $30 · Steady $35 · Onward $40 · Full journey $100 · Cohort of 10 ≈ $1,000. Confirm final amounts against the planner.
Where
A Sponsor a pack page, and as a "Sponsor this pack" action beside each pack on The packs page.
Nice touch
Let a sponsor add a short dedication or "in memory of" note — it doubles the meaning and, with permission, gives you gentle future stories.
Embed / link — to create in ZeffyBuild as a Zeffy shop; mirror the live donation form's branding. Placeholder: https://www.zeffy.com/en-AU/ticketing/sponsor-a-klaire-pack

New page — Transparency

Because gifts aren't tax-deductible, showing exactly where money goes is your strongest trust signal.

A short, plain page that answers three questions a thoughtful donor asks: what does a pack actually cost, how much has been raised and spent, and where does my money go? The numbers come straight from your live cost planner, so the page is honest by construction.

Welcome pack
$33

$38
Onward pack
$99
All three together

Current planning estimates from the cost sheet — the page should show live figures, not hard-coded ones.

How to build it
  • Simplest, recommended: embed a clean, public summary view of the Google Sheet (funds raised, funds spent, cost per box, a one-line "where it goes"). It's always current because it is the sheet — no manual updates on the site.
  • Publish it safely: in Google Sheets use File → Share → Publish to web and publish only a dedicated clean summary tab, then embed that. Don't expose the full working planner — it contains supplier notes and every backup item, which is more than the public needs.
  • Optional upgrade: a light dashboard-style layout on the page (a few big numbers + a simple bar for cost-per-box) that reads the published sheet. Nice-to-have, not needed for launch.
  • Always include one honest sentence: that Klaire Packs is a not-for-profit and income only ever goes back into packs, postage and running costs — never to profit or private benefit.
Data source — cost planner (private working sheet)docs.google.com/spreadsheets/…/1FykPm85O1HdUZzYmy6jULVQ5jzmSBl_NgP1EvMUbfSI
Consultant note: resist the urge to publish precise "raised so far" figures if they're small early on — a modest number can undercut confidence. It's fine to launch with cost-per-box and the "where it goes" breakdown, and add running totals once they tell a good story.
07

New page — Screening Rules

The page that answers a centre's first question — "is this safe for our setting?" — before they ask it.

A plain, reassuring page stating the safety rules every pack is screened against. It reassures centres, and it quietly reassures donors that this is done with real care. Content comes from the Screening Rules tab of the planner, which draws on the Klaire Packs Gift Acceptance Note (Appendix A) and published clinic guidance. Frame it as the strictest baseline — always confirmed with each centre's own prohibited-items list.

Never included — hard exclusions
  • Any medicine, vitamin, mineral or supplement, and anything containing alcohol (including mouthwash, sanitiser, or lotions listing "alcohol denat.").
  • Nuts and any "may contain nuts" product; poppy seeds, hemp seeds and CBD — all can affect a drug screen and risk someone's place in the program.
  • Diet / "detox" / "skinny" teas and laxative blends; soft drinks and energy drinks.
  • Aerosols and sprays, candles and incense (fire and smoke-alarm risk).
  • Sharps of any kind — scissors, needles, razors, blades or metal points, including hidden ones inside craft kits.
  • Devices with a camera, WiFi or Bluetooth; hairdryers and straighteners; any liquid or full drink bottle.
  • Cash inside a pack; used, opened, expired or unlabelled goods; anything needing refrigeration; and violent, offensive, political, religious or adult content.
Confirmed clinic-by-clinic
  • Scented items, wheat / microwavable heat bags, and gift cards or vouchers.
  • Long cords or drawstrings (a ligature-risk flag), spiral binding, and pencil sharpeners.
  • Items intended for residents' children on site.
How we screen
  • Every item is checked against this baseline and the individual centre's prohibited-items list before the first order.
  • The full contents list is sent to the centre in advance for approval.
  • Packs are never sealed in a way that makes the centre's own inspection destructive.
Data source — "Screening Rules" tab of the cost plannerdocs.google.com/spreadsheets/…/1FykPm85O1HdUZzYmy6jULVQ5jzmSBl_NgP1EvMUbfSI
Design note: give this page the sage accent (not terracotta) — it's the "facility-safe" colour, ties to the pack artwork, and visually separates the reassurance pages from the commercial ones.

How the Zeffy embeds actually work

Notes for the build.

  • Embed pattern. Each Zeffy form is an <iframe>. Wrap it in a responsive container with a sensible min-height, and always put a plain link beneath it — "Having trouble? Open the form in a new tab →." Some browsers, extensions and — importantly — locked-down clinic networks block third-party iframes; the fallback keeps the form working.
  • No payment data on-site. All card entry, processing and receipts happen inside Zeffy. The site only hosts a link or iframe — no PCI or card-handling burden.
  • Confirmations. Zeffy emails the supporter and can notify Klaire Packs per submission. Where possible, set the post-submit redirect back to a simple "thank you" page on the site.
  • Keep it seamless. Brand the Zeffy forms — logo, terracotta accent, same voice — so moving from site to form doesn't feel like leaving.
10

GitHub Pages — what to design around

  • Static only. No server code, no database, no server-side login. Everything dynamic is delegated to Zeffy (and the Transparency data to a published Google Sheet). This is why the site can be free and near-zero-maintenance.
  • Custom domain. Point klairepacks.org at GitHub Pages via a CNAME and switch on "Enforce HTTPS."
  • Clinic gating. Unlisted link + manual approval, not authentication (as in §5.3).
  • Forms & email. No server means no self-hosted forms — use Zeffy (or a form service) for anything that collects data, so submissions arrive by email.
  • Analytics (optional). If you want visitor numbers, use a light, cookieless, privacy-first tool — kinder to visitors and keeps the site fast.
  • Housekeeping. Rename Claire → Klaire across every page, the wordmark, logo alt text and the contact address (hello@klairepacks.org); add page titles/descriptions for search and social sharing; keep it fully responsive and keyboard-navigable — clinic staff and older donors both matter.
11

Trust, compliance & tone

The non-negotiables — where a small NFP earns or loses a donor's confidence.

Not tax-deductible — say it kindly

Klaire Packs isn't a DGR, so no donation or sponsorship is tax-deductible. Never use "tax-deductible," "claim it back," or a receipt that mimics an ATO gift receipt. A warm, plain line builds more trust than silence: "We're a small not-for-profit, not a registered charity, so gifts aren't tax-deductible — but every dollar goes straight into packs." The Transparency page (§06) backs this up with real numbers.

Be clear where the money goes

One honest sentence about the not-for-profit character — income only ever goes back into the packs and the work — reassures donors more than any badge or seal.

Facility-safe, front and centre

Every item is unscented where required, non-restricted, nothing sharp, and screened against each centre's prohibited-items list. The Screening Rules page (§07) makes this visible; answer the question before it's asked.

Privacy on every form & care with the story

Keep the one-line privacy note the alpha already uses ("No mailing lists"). And handle Klaire's story with care: let the founder own the exact words, and keep the illustrative centre quotes clearly labelled until real, consented ones replace them.
